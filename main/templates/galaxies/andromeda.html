🌀 Andromeda

Our Closest Spiral Galaxy Neighbor

What is Andromeda?

Andromeda Galaxy is the closest large galaxy to the Milky Way.

Its light takes about 2.5 million years to reach Earth.

Even though it looks small in the sky, it contains trillions of stars.

A Giant Spiral Galaxy

Andromeda is a spiral galaxy with long rotating arms.

Main Features

  • Bright central core
  • Huge spiral arms
  • Dense star clusters
  • Dust and gas clouds

It looks similar to the Milky Way, but is even larger.

How Big is Andromeda?

  • About 220,000 light-years wide
  • Contains over 1 trillion stars
  • Larger than the Milky Way

Even at light speed, crossing it would take over 200,000 years.

How Far Away is It?

Andromeda is about:

🌌 2.5 million light-years from Earth

Looking at Andromeda means seeing the distant past, long before humans existed.

Future Collision with the Milky Way

Scientists believe Andromeda and the Milky Way will slowly collide in about 4–5 billion years.

Most stars probably will not crash directly, because space is extremely empty.

The galaxies may merge into one giant galaxy sometimes called:

🌌 Milkomeda

Why is Andromeda Important?

  • Helps study galaxy evolution
  • Shows gravity on massive scales
  • Reveals the future of our galaxy

Conclusion

Andromeda is a giant neighboring galaxy slowly moving through the universe beside us.

It helps scientists understand the structure and future of cosmic systems.
