🌌 The Milky Way

Our Home Galaxy in Space

What is the Milky Way?

Milky Way is the galaxy where our Solar System exists. It is a huge collection of stars, planets, gas, dust, and dark matter held together by gravity.

From Earth, we see a faint glowing band across the night sky — that is part of the Milky Way.

What Does the Milky Way Look Like?

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy, meaning it has a central bar-shaped structure with spiral arms extending outward.

Main Features

  • A bright central bulge packed with stars
  • Long spiral arms full of gas and star clusters
  • A thin rotating disk
  • A surrounding halo of older stars

Our Solar System is located in one of these spiral arms called the Orion Arm.

How Big is the Milky Way?

  • About 100,000–120,000 light-years wide
  • Contains 100–400 billion stars
  • May contain even more planets than stars

Even at the speed of light, it would take over 100,000 years to travel across it.

What is at the Center?

At the center of the Milky Way is a supermassive black hole:

⚫ Sagittarius A*

This black hole has a mass millions of times greater than the Sun. It strongly influences nearby stars and galactic movement.

Where Are We in the Galaxy?

  • About 27,000 light-years from the center
  • Inside the Orion Arm
  • Far from dangerous radiation zones

This location helps life exist safely on Earth.

How Does the Milky Way Move?

The Milky Way constantly moves through space.

  • Rotates slowly over millions of years
  • Moves with nearby galaxies
  • Will collide with Andromeda in the distant future

Why is the Milky Way Important?

Studying our galaxy helps scientists understand:

  • How galaxies evolve
  • How stars are born and die
  • How black holes grow
  • Our place in the universe

Conclusion

The Milky Way is a giant rotating galaxy containing everything we know — including Earth, the Sun, and all life.

Understanding it helps humanity understand the universe itself.
